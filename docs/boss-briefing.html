QUESTION 02

How do we convince people who already pay for another tool?

You asked: “How do I convince those who already subscribed to other tools — what unique thing are we giving?”

We don’t ask them to switch. We sell a different purchase for a different job. Their subscription tells them what the law says; ours produces what they have to file. Keeping Taxmann and adding BharatTax is the natural position, not an either/or.

We already subscribe to Taxmann.
Keep it. Taxmann tells you what the law says; BharatTax drafts your order, watches your limitation dates and computes your interest. And on the assessment / recovery / investigation side, Taxmann doesn’t serve you at all — we do.
Why not just use ChatGPT?
It will confidently cite a section that’s been amended or never existed — you can’t put that before a bench — and it won’t draft your 143(3) order or flag a time-bar. Ours is cited, and honest when it doesn’t know.
How do we trust an AI with our case data?
Because your case data stays on your infrastructure. Taxmann never had to solve this; ChatGPT can’t. Only we keep the working data under your control — see Question 03.
QUESTION 03

The government wants the data on their premises, not the cloud.

You raised: “Government data is very important — they want all of it kept at their place. They won’t be happy saving their data in the cloud.”
Deployment hook live in production

Correct — and we don’t fight it, we offer it. Sovereignty does not mean a server in every office. It means the data sits on government-controlled infrastructure — one central instance, exactly like ITBA, Insight and TRACES already run. Officers connect over the department network and manage nothing.

  • Managed sovereign instance (default for govt): a dedicated, isolated instance on gov-approved Indian infrastructure — their tenant, their keys — that we operate. On-prem-grade control, SaaS-grade convenience, zero ops burden on any office.
  • Central-hosted or air-gapped on-prem: for the highest-control wings, the whole stack and a local AI model run inside the department’s network. Nothing leaves — no byte to any outside cloud.
  • What we shipped this month: one build now points at any backend by editing a single config file on their server — no rebuild — so “runs on your infrastructure” is a demo, not a claim. The local-AI-model path is already a first-class option in the product.

Honest note for the room: an on-network AI model needs the department’s own GPU to match cloud quality. We offer a hybrid — the sensitive drafting stays local; general research can use a government-approved managed model where policy permits.

QUESTION 04

Personalisation only worked inside one chat. It should carry everywhere.

You raised: “I tested it — personalisation is limited only to the current chat. I want it to be overall, interconnected across all things.”
Fixed & live across chat and drafted orders

You were right, and it was a genuine gap — the officer’s profile only reached a fallback path, not the live product. We found it, fixed it, and shipped it. Personalisation is now platform-wide.

  • Every answer, every mode: the officer’s role, jurisdiction, their own instructions and durable cross-chat memory now feed the live chat — single-agent, multi-agent and document Q&A alike — not just one conversation.
  • Connected to their caseload: answers are aware of the officer’s open matters and their nearest deadline — the tool knows the desk it sits on.
  • Now reaches the drafts too (shipped this week): the assessment order, the appellate order and the CASS questionnaire are framed for the officer’s own charge and house style — carefully walled so an order still rests only on that case’s record.
  • Next: deepen it wing-by-wing and role-by-role, so each designation’s desk, defaults and drafting are tuned to that officer’s actual work.

Demo in one minute: sign in, set a charge and an instruction in the profile, ask a question — the answer adopts them — then generate a draft and see the same identity on the order.

A private AI that lives inside the department, drafts the officer’s orders and computes their deadlines — every line cited — and now knows each officer across their whole desk.
